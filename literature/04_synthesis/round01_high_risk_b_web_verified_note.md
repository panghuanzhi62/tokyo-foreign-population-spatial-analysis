## A. Purpose

This note records three high-risk B_partial_overlap candidates from Round 01
that have been verified through publisher and academic metadata sources (DOI,
publisher pages, and institutional/index records), and explains why they are
now entered into literature_matrix.csv and gap_verification_matrix.csv. Web /
metadata verification is sufficient for bibliographic confirmation of these
three items, so immediate SciSpace review is not required for them; SciSpace may
still be used later if full-text interpretation is needed.

## C. Overlap assessment

### R01_HRB_001 (Liu-Farrer 2013)

- Overlaps with: Chinese migration to Japan and disaster (2011 earthquake)
  context.
- Does not cover: an integrated Metropolitan Tokyo analysis combining housing,
  transit/accessibility, services, disaster exposure, and an opportunity-risk
  typology; no non-Chinese comparison; spatial unit not defined.
- Not coded A_direct_overlap because it is a migration-profile and
  earthquake-impact discussion, not a combined spatial opportunity-risk study of
  Metropolitan Tokyo.
- Full-text reading may still help confirm exactly how disaster impact and any
  settlement/housing dimensions are treated.

### R01_HRB_002 (Liang 2025)

- Overlaps with: residential segregation and ethnic stratification (housing
  module) in Japan; the strongest theoretical/empirical comparator of the three.
- Does not cover (on current metadata): transit/accessibility, services,
  disaster exposure, or an opportunity-risk mismatch typology; the degree of
  Chinese-specific focus and any non-Chinese comparison is unclear from metadata.
- Not coded A_direct_overlap because, although it sits close to the segregation /
  spatial-assimilation line (and to Sun 2026), it does not appear to integrate
  the full provisional-topic module set.
- Full-text reading is recommended to confirm the population groups, spatial
  unit, data source, and whether Chinese residents are separately analyzed.

### R01_HRB_003 (Liu-Farrer 2009)

- Overlaps with: Chinese student migration and education-labor mobility from
  China to Japan (background / arrival-pathway context).
- Does not cover: housing, accessibility, services, disaster, or opportunity-risk
  mismatch; no non-Chinese comparison; not a Tokyo spatial study.
- Not coded A_direct_overlap (closer to E_background_only) because it explains
  migration channels rather than the lived settlement environment in Tokyo.
- Full-text reading is optional and mainly useful for arrival-infrastructure
  framing.

## D. Cross-item conclusion
