## C. One-paragraph provisional positioning

At the current metadata and source-verification stage, the project provisionally
positions itself as a public-data urban-geography study of how Chinese residents
are situated within opportunity-risk environments in Metropolitan Tokyo,
compared with non-Chinese foreign residents, across housing, accessibility,
service infrastructure, and disaster/risk exposure. Audit-stage evidence suggests
that existing Japanese-source records provide partial support for the
Chinese-resident, housing, residential-concentration, and service-context
components, but the audit has not yet identified a confirmed full-combination
direct-overlap record (0 of 43 audited records), and the Chinese-vs-non-Chinese
comparison and integrated opportunity-risk typology remain weakly covered. This
positioning is provisional and requires further full-text and translation checks
before any formal claim.

## D. Boundary with Sun 2026

Per sun_2026_positioning_note.md and the audit table, Sun 2026 (Cities) already
covers:
- immigrant location choice in the Tokyo metropolitan area (242 municipalities,
  2012-2018),
- spatial assimilation,
- co-ethnic networks,
- Chinese and other immigrant groups (Vietnamese, Filipino, Brazilian).

Therefore the project should NOT be framed as:
- a Chinese co-ethnic-network study,
- a Chinese immigrant location-choice study,
- a spatial-assimilation study,
- a study filling a gap in Chinese migrant settlement in Tokyo in general.

The safer positioning is:
- an opportunity-risk ENVIRONMENTAL PROFILE;
- Chinese residents COMPARED WITH non-Chinese foreign residents;
- PUBLIC-DATA CONSTRUCTION across housing, accessibility, services, and
  disaster/risk exposure;
- METROPOLITAN-SCALE SPATIAL DIFFERENTIATION rather than migration-network
  mechanism.

## G. Recommended topic wording (internal, provisional)

All titles below are INTERNAL and PROVISIONAL; none asserts novelty.

1. Conservative academic title:
   "Chinese Residents and Opportunity-Risk Environments in Metropolitan Tokyo: A
   Public-Data Analysis of Housing, Accessibility, Services, and Disaster
   Exposure"
2. Methods/data-oriented title:
   "Profiling Opportunity-Risk Environments of Chinese and Non-Chinese Foreign
   Residents in Metropolitan Tokyo Using Public Spatial and Administrative Data"
3. Application/outreach-friendly title:
   "Where Do Chinese Residents Live in Metropolitan Tokyo? A Public-Data Look at
   Housing, Access, Services, and Disaster Risk"

Terminology guidance:
- Use "Chinese nationals" or "Chinese residents by nationality" in DATA sections
  when official Japanese data classify by nationality (e-Stat, ISA 在留外国人統計,
  Basic Resident Register).
- Use "Chinese residents" / "Chinese-origin residents" carefully in the broader
  migration-literature framing, and clarify the nationality-vs-origin distinction
  explicitly (data are nationality-based; some literature uses origin/ethnicity).
- The KEY comparison is "Chinese residents vs non-Chinese foreign residents".
  "All foreign residents" should be used only as a BACKGROUND BENCHMARK, not as
  the primary contrast.

## H. Provisional research contribution statement (internal)

The project may contribute by shifting attention from migration-network or
spatial-assimilation MECHANISMS (already addressed by Sun 2026 and related work)
toward the PUBLIC-DATA PROFILING of opportunity-risk ENVIRONMENTS, combining
housing, accessibility, service infrastructure, and disaster/risk exposure into
a single metropolitan-scale comparison of Chinese versus non-Chinese foreign
residents. The audited records support each component partially but do not
integrate them, which is what makes the integrated profiling angle provisionally
distinctive.

This contribution remains PROVISIONAL until full-text reading and translation
checks of the priority records are completed and until official-data licensing
and usability are confirmed. No final novelty or gap claim should be attached to
it at this stage; status remains gap under verification.

## K. Provisional research questions

1. How are Chinese residents (by nationality) distributed across opportunity-risk
   environments in Metropolitan Tokyo, defined by housing, accessibility, service
   infrastructure, and disaster/risk exposure?
2. How do these opportunity-risk environments compare with those of non-Chinese
   foreign residents (with all foreign residents as a background benchmark)?
3. Which municipalities or neighborhoods combine housing opportunity, service
   access, and disaster/risk exposure in patterns that indicate an
   opportunity-risk mismatch?
4. How do available official data sources support or constrain the construction of
   such a public-data opportunity-risk profile?

(Descriptive/associational framing only; no causal claims unless later justified.)
